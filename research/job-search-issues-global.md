# Global Job Search Barriers: APAC, LatAm, Africa & Cross-Border Remote Work

Companion to `research/job-search-issues-us-eu.md` (Task A). Covers emerging markets and
cross-border hiring. All sources accessed 2026-09-20; full ledger with per-source metadata in
`work/t_3c9ed8d3/ledger.json`. Sources marked ⚠ are practitioner/SEO content — corroborated
where possible, lower trust than institutional sources.

## 1. Structural Barriers

* **Formal-hiring cost walls (LatAm)**: Employers pay ~50% more in non-wage labor costs than in
  wages (social security contributions + labor regulation). This prices formal jobs out of reach:
  informality spans 48% of workers across 7 major LAC economies and ~55% across 27 LAC countries
  (OECD 2025). A "job search" in much of LatAm is largely a search for *formal* contracts that
  structurally don't get created. (ILO Labour Overview 2025; IDB; OECD)
* **Job creation without quality (LatAm)**: LAC generated ~27M net new jobs 2016–2024 with the
  lowest regional GDP growth since 2015 — low-productivity work dominates. Youth unemployment is
  14% (double the overall rate); NEET rate 18.3% (World Bank Regional Jobs Update, May 2025).
* **Informal/gig economy as the default market (Africa & LatAm)**: In Africa the informal sector
  has been the main employment-growth absorber for decades; the realistic job market is multiple
  gigs with "somewhat formal" entities, not salaried positions (CGD). ~46M Latin Americans do
  platform gig work; platforms act as brokers of the informal economy (PCMI). Resume-and-ATS
  tooling assumes a formal job market that most candidates aren't actually in.
* **Talent shortage paradox (APAC)**: 77% of APAC employers couldn't fill roles in 2025 (81% in
  IT/telecom); by 2026, 71% across APME with Japan worst at 84%, India 82%, Singapore 71% — yet
  China sits at only 48%. Shortage coexists with hard entry for candidates: screening is
  credential- and channel-driven, not capacity-driven. (ManpowerGroup 2025/2026)
* **Discrimination outside legal enforcement reach (APAC)**: Gender (South Asia: women hold only
  16.5% of non-agricultural paid jobs; 2/3 of employed women work unpaid), caste (Dalits excluded
  from food-handling, domestic and service work), ethnicity, religion and health status
  (HIV+ workers report workplace discrimination; Philippines 21%) shape hiring — mostly in
  markets with weak anti-discrimination enforcement. Malaysia: qualification inflation plus
  openly discriminatory ad requirements (gender/age) persisted until platforms began blocking
  them; interview-stage discretion remains. (ILO Asia fact sheet; Staffing Industry Analysts ⚠
  2013, dated — use for pattern, not current state)
* **Unique structural hiring systems (Japan/Korea)**: *Shinsotsu ikkatsu saiyou* — synchronized
  annual batch-hiring of new graduates with fixed timelines, entry sheets, SPI tests and
  university-affiliation (*gakureki*) screening (now covert). Foreign graduates face the same
  machine plus language tests and visa-status changes, with scarce support services. In Korea,
  chaebol preference for experienced hires created chronic youth underemployment (7.0% youth
  unemployment, June 2026) and pushed Korean graduates toward Japan's graduate schemes (Reuters).
  (JASSO; Mainichi; tokyo.how ⚠; ejcjs/Kawanishi; Reuters)
* **Visas as gatekeeper (cross-border)**: Since 2022 ~55% of jurisdictions tightened work-visa
  eligibility (salary thresholds, skills tests, caps); US H-1B cap cut 15% in FY2024 plus
  points-style weighting; EU Blue Card demands multi-year contracts and wage floors ≥30% above
  national median in 12 member states; UK requires sponsor licences with rising salary minimums.
  Meanwhile 71% of multinationals allow routine cross-border remote work (up from 58% in 2019) —
  legal work authorization, not demand, is the binding constraint. (CareerAhead ⚠ for the
  aggregate numbers; corroborated per-route by gov.uk-based practitioner guides ⚠)
* **Recruitment-fee migration economy (South Asia → Gulf/SEA)**: Contract migration runs on
  upfront fees: Nepali workers paid an average NPR 100,000 (~US$1,400) — ~3× average annual
  income — often financed at up to 60% annual interest; 90% of surveyed migrants were deceived
  about contracts (wages, conditions, rest days). Passport confiscation is routine. The "job
  offer" itself is the risky asset. (Amnesty International; IOM on transnational recruitment
  corruption)
* **Employer-of-Record bypass (remote LatAm/Africa/Asia)**: EOR platforms (Deel, Remote, Oyster)
  let foreign firms hire compliantly without local entities — expanding cross-border remote
  access for emerging-market professionals while sidestepping local formal-hiring cost walls
  (see LatAm above). (jobsrmine ⚠)

## 2. Behavioral Friction

* **More applications, fewer callbacks (Singapore/APAC)**: 6 in 10 Singapore workers say job
  search got harder year-over-year (vs 5 in 10 global); 47% report applying to more jobs and
  receiving fewer callbacks; professionals spend 1–2 hrs/week submitting up to 5–8 applications
  weekly. Same application-volume treadmill as the US/EU, in Asia's most digitalized market.
  (LinkedIn data via Straits Times)
* **Channel-mismatch for informal workers**: Job-matching infrastructure (portals, ATS, LinkedIn)
  targets formal-sector candidates. Informal and gig workers in LatAm/Africa find work through
  networks, walk-ins and platforms — the dominant search behaviors aren't served by the tools
  built for the formal market. (J-PAL on search frictions & quality-job matching; IDB on weak
  public employment services/ALMPs)
* **Youth skills-program churn (Africa)**: Training programs across Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya,
  Niger, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa, Uganda fail as stand-alone interventions —
  small-scale, poorly targeted, disconnected from actual demand. Young people cycle through
  programs without employment; first-fired/last-hired during downturns. (The Conversation;
  GSDRC helpdesk)
* **Network-gated hiring (Africa)**: "No connections, no employment" — youth graduate
  unemployment in South Africa is materially mediated by social capital; referrals and
  acquaintance networks gate access that credentials alone don't unlock. Echoes referral-based
  hiring everywhere but with far lower formal-channel availability. (EHASS 2025; GSDRC)
* **Sequential single-shot systems (Japan)**: The batch-hiring calendar makes job hunting a
  one-shot contest synchronized nationally; missing the window or failing once costs a full
  year. Foreign students additionally burn time decoding the system itself (rirekisho/ES norms,
  SPI, visa timing Dec–Jan). (JASSO; tokyo.how ⚠)
* **Cross-border application overload**: International candidates navigate per-country visa
  matrices, credential equivalence, salary-threshold eligibility and EOR-vs-relocation routes —
  each application carries compliance research overhead locals never see. (jobsrmine ⚠;
  CareerAhead ⚠)

## 3. Candidate Experience / Emotional Toll

* **Scam-centre trafficking via fake job offers (Africa → SEA)**: The most severe outcome in
  global job search: candidates from Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia (and 50+ countries) are recruited
  through plausible offers — customer-service and tech roles, staged interviews, paid travel —
  then trafficked into fortified scam compounds in Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos and the Philippines,
  forced to run romance/crypto scams under violence. 74% of known victims trafficked into scam
  centres 2020–2025 went through Southeast Asia. A Kenyan candidate sold his cattle and paid a
  "recruitment agency" £1,150 before being routed to Myanmar. (UNODC #TrappedInScamCrime;
  The Guardian)
* **Fee-extraction job scams (Africa)**: Bogus postings impersonating UN agencies and NGOs on
  LinkedIn/Facebook charge "facilitation," "medical test" and "training" fees (Kenya: 1.6M+
  unemployed youth = target pool); harvest personal data for identity theft and blackmail. UN
  agencies issue recurring warnings; police reports rarely resolve. (AFP Fact Check)
* **Recruitment debt bondage (South Asia)**: Migration-for-work financed by usurious loans
  against deceptive contracts leaves workers trapped abroad — wages below promise, passports
  held, no rest days. Women face additional channel restrictions that push them into
  undocumented migration with worse abuse exposure (21-hr days documented). (Amnesty
  International)
* **Shūkatsu utsu — job-hunting depression (Japan)**: A named clinical phenomenon: depression
  triggered by the job-hunting ritual itself. Mass batch hiring + covert *gakureki* (university
  rank) filtering + "life is a one-shot contest" framing produces hopelessness among college
  seniors; foreign graduates report analogous anxiety without support networks. (ejcjs/Kawanishi;
  Mainichi)
* **Ghosting in mature APAC markets**: 48% of Singapore respondents ghosted by recruiters;
  rejection opacity ("didn't know why") mirrors US/EU ghosting findings — behavioral friction
  transfers across markets faster than process fixes. (LinkedIn via Straits Times)
* **Discrimination as chronic attrition (APAC)**: Candidates facing gender/caste/religious
  filtering absorb repeated, unexplained rejections with no legal recourse or feedback —
  compounding the emotional cost of already-extended searches. (ILO Asia fact sheet)

## 4. Platform Opportunities (brief mapping only)

* **Scam/phantom-offer verification**: Red-flag detection for postings that charge fees at any
  stage, impersonate institutions, or route through personal channels — the highest-stakes
  opportunity in emerging markets (UNODC/UNEP publish explicit "no legitimate fee" rules that
  are mechanically checkable). Directly extends the phantom-job detection opportunity identified
  in `job-search-issues-us-eu.md`.
* **Informal/gig-first profiles**: Candidate profiles that value platform work history, gig
  reputation and portfolio evidence over formal employment continuity — serves the ~50%
  informality baseline in LatAm/Africa instead of assuming an ATS-shaped career.
* **Timeline & system decoding**: Region-specific hiring-calendar guidance (Japan's shinsotsu
  window, Korea's experienced-hire bias, EU/US loops) and expectation-setting, mirroring the
  "market-aware pacing" opportunity from Task A but keyed to batch-hiring systems rather than
  interview-loop counts.
* **Network-replacement signaling**: Structured, verifiable evidence of skills for candidates
  without connections or referral access (South Africa's "no connections" gap; Africa's
  youth-program churn) — lowers the referral dependence that informal-channel hiring runs on.
* **Cross-border eligibility triage**: Salary-threshold/visa-route pre-checks (which doors can
  this candidate actually open: relocation, EOR remote, digital-nomad) before application effort
  is spent. Adjacent to Task A's ATS-translation opportunity; different constraint (law vs
  keyword filters).
* **Interview-language rehearsal**: Practice layer for English-interview conventions (self-
  quantified answers, directness norms) that screen out skilled LATAM/SEA candidates despite
  technical fit — cross-border interview failure is cultural-linguistic, not competence-based.
  (HiredRemote ⚠)

## Cross-market synthesis (with `job-search-issues-us-eu.md`)

| Barrier | US/EU | Emerging markets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Screening filter | ATS keyword/algorithm | *Gakureki* rank, credentials inflation, cast/gender/religion bias |
| Ghosting/opacity | Employer ghosting post-interview | Same + unexplained discrimination-based rejection |
| Structural gatekeeper | Labor law & at-will churn, RTO | Formal-hiring cost (LatAm), batch-hiring calendar (Japan), visa regimes (cross-border) |
| Scam exposure | Phantom jobs harvesting data | Fee-extraction scams + trafficking-grade fake offers |
| Emotional toll | Burnout, application fatigue | Named clinical phenomena (shūkatsu utsu), debt bondage, physical danger |
| Referral dependence | High (employee referrals) | Extreme (social capital as near-prerequisite in SA/Africa) |

Shared invariant across all markets studied: candidates with the least slack pay the highest
friction, and every market's dominant filter (ATS keyword, university rank, network, visa,
formal contract) is invisible to the candidate before they apply.
